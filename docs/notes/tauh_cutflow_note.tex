\title{\vspace{-1.5cm}Delphes vs.\ CMS for HH$\rightarrow b\bar b\tau\tau$:\\
\large where we stand, and one decision to take}
\author{}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The $\tautau$ yield deficit that motivated this work (a factor $\sim$5) is closed: the
cutflow agrees with CMS at every stage ($1.01$) and eight of the ten NSBI features agree
within statistics. Two do not --- $\mtautau$ and $\dR_{\tau\tau}$ --- and both are on the
$\tau$ side. The $\mtautau$ offset has now been traced: it is in the $\tau$ four-vectors
rather than the mass estimator or the $\ptmiss$, fake $\tauh$ are excluded as its cause by
measurement, and all of it sits in the \emph{gen-matched} population. Since the
energy-scale map is a per-$p_{T}$ \emph{median} ratio, it can align the peak but cannot
reshape a response distribution --- so the residual may lie outside what the correction
approach can reach at all. That would turn the open decision (Section~\ref{sec:decision})
from a question of elegance into one of feasibility. The response distribution has now been
measured, with the $\tau$ ID required on both sides: Delphes carries a one-sided tail that
CMS does not, and it is $p_{T}$-dependent in the way underlying event inside the $R=0.4$
cone would make it (Section~\ref{sec:response}). At gen $p_{T}=20$--$30\,\si{\GeV}$ one
Delphes $\tau$ in ten carries more than twice the median energy response, against $1.08$ on
CMS. That closes off the \emph{current} correction --- a multiplicative median cannot
reshape a distribution --- but it leaves three options rather than two, since a
\emph{distributional} map could (Section~\ref{sec:decision}).
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Known limitations, accepted for now.} None of these is implicated in the two
disagreements above, but they bound what the samples can be used for.
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{The $\tauh$ fake rate is measured on signal.} \texttt{tau\_mistag} comes
from the $b$-rich HH anchor. Fake rates are strongly jet-flavour dependent, so this value
is \emph{not} appropriate for the fake-dominated backgrounds (QCD, W+jets), which need
their own measurement before they are trusted.
\item \textbf{$\ptmiss$ smearing is isotropic and flat}, where the real resolution is
anisotropic and grows with $\Sigma E_{T}$. This reproduces the width but not its
structure.
\item \textbf{Pileup enters only through $\ptmiss$.} The card runs without pileup, so jet
energy \emph{resolution} is untuned and pileup jets cannot be produced at all. Both act on
the $b\bar b$ side, which agrees; the second cannot be fixed downstream in any case.
\item \textbf{Two defects in the energy-scale application}, real but not the cause of the
$\mtautau$ offset: fake $\tauh$ match neither the $b$ nor the $\tau$ branch of
\texttt{escale\_factor} and are left uncorrected while still receiving a $\tau$-like mass;
and the $\tau$ branch matches \emph{all} gen $\tau$ (\texttt{|pid|==15}) including
leptonic ones, whereas the map is derived on the hadronic visible set.
\item \textbf{The sampled $\tauh$ mass is drawn independently} of the $\tau$ energy and
of the partner leg, whereas the true visible mass correlates with the decay mode and hence
with prong multiplicity and energy sharing. Unmeasured; distinct from the quantile-grid
defect below, which \emph{was} measured.
\item \textbf{FastMTT is covariance-free} (a fixed $\ptmiss$ resolution rather than the
per-event covariance). Run identically on both sides, so it largely cancels in the
comparison; it limits the absolute $\mtautau$ resolution, not the agreement.
\item \textbf{Every map is flat-extrapolated above $300\,\si{\GeV}$}, the last bin edge.
Untested, and worth checking: $\kl$ discrimination lives at high $m_{HH}$ and therefore at
high jet $p_{T}$.
\end{itemize}

\section{The two open questions}

\subsection{$\mtautau$: the offset is in the $\tau$ four-vectors}
\label{sec:mtautau}

Because $\mtautau=\mvis/\sqrt{x_{1}x_{2}}$ factorises, the \emph{visible} di-$\tau$ mass
$\mvis$ --- built from the two selected $\tau$ four-vectors, with no $\ptmiss$ and no
FastMTT --- separates a $\tau$ energy-scale error from an error in the $x$ fit. The two
have disjoint fixes, so it is worth measuring before tuning anything.

\paragraph{What is measured.} On the $\kl=0$ overlay, $\mvis$ is offset by \emph{at least}
as much as $\mtautau$ is (peak ratios $\approx1.24$ against $\approx1.17$, read off the
histograms). The $\tau$ four-vectors therefore carry the discrepancy and the FastMTT fit
is not its source; if anything it compresses it. Both $\tau$ legs are individually harder
on Delphes. Consistently, with an applied scale of $\simeq0.85$ and a residual ratio of
$\simeq1.24$, the raw Delphes $\tau$ are $\sim46\%$ harder than CMS while the map corrects
only $15\%$.

Splitting into gen-matched and fake components then \textbf{excludes fakes}: the fake
fractions are $4.1\%$ on Delphes against $4.3\%$ on CMS with indistinguishable shapes,
while the gen-matched component carries the whole discrepancy --- both the peak
displacement and an unphysical tail at $\mvis>125\,\si{\GeV}$, which is kinematically
impossible for $H\rightarrow\tau\tau$ and which CMS does not populate. The same pattern
holds for $\dR_{\tau\tau}$ (Section~\ref{sec:drtautau}); both $\tau$-side disagreements
live in \emph{real} $\tau$.

\paragraph{Why this may be a statement about the representation.}
\texttt{tau\_escale} is a per-$p_{T}$-bin \emph{median} ratio applied multiplicatively. By
construction it aligns medians; it cannot change the \emph{shape} of the response
distribution. An $R=0.4$ cone absorbs underlying event and nearby particles for some
$\tau$, where an HPS signal cone would not, producing a one-sided high tail in the
response. Such a tail is not reachable by any multiplicative median map --- one can move
the peak into place and still be left with entries in a kinematically forbidden region,
because a scale factor cannot depopulate one. If that is what is happening, it is a hard
floor on the correction approach rather than a defect in its implementation.

\paragraph{The response distribution: measured.}
\label{sec:response}
Plotting the $\tau$ energy response --- reco $\tau$ $p_{T}$ over gen-visible-$\tau$ $p_{T}$
--- as a \emph{distribution} rather than a per-bin median, with each side divided by its
\emph{own} median so the scale is removed by construction, leaves only what the map cannot
change. One control is essential first: without a $\tau$ ID the two sides are not matched
symmetrically, since Delphes matches each gen $\tau$ to the nearest jet of \emph{any} kind
--- so a $\tau$ whose own $\tau$-jet was lost can match a nearby $b$-jet and give a spurious
response --- whereas CMS matches within the \texttt{Tau} collection and cannot pick up a
$b$-jet at all. Mis-matching would also mimic the $p_{T}$ dependence, soft $\tau$-jets being
more often lost. With the ID required on both sides (Delphes \texttt{TauTag}, CMS DeepTau
Medium) the tail survives:

CMS is flat in $p_{T}$ ($1.10$--$1.14$ throughout), as a well-calibrated object with roughly
constant fractional resolution should be; its $q_{90}$/median of $1.076$ implies
$\sigma\simeq6\%$, the right order for HPS $\tau$, so the anchor itself looks sound. Delphes
is not flat, and converges on CMS as $p_{T}$ rises --- exactly what a roughly constant
\emph{absolute} energy excess produces, being a large fraction at $25\,\si{\GeV}$ and a
small one at $175$. The medians tell the same story and converge alongside, as a single
cause would predict. The sharpest single number: at $20$--$30\,\si{\GeV}$ the Delphes
$q_{90}$/median is $2.156$ --- one $\tau$ in ten carrying more than twice the median
response, against $1.076$ on CMS. That is not a resolution.

One caveat survives the control. Delphes' \texttt{TauTag} is a stochastic draw uncorrelated
with contamination, while DeepTau is isolation-based and actively rejects contaminated
candidates; so part of the difference is a \emph{selection} correlation rather than a cone
effect. It is equally unreachable by a scale map, so it does not change the conclusion
below, but it does mean the tail is not purely a statement about cone size.

\paragraph{What has been excluded.} \emph{(i) The sampled $\tauh$ mass.} The FastMTT floor
$x_{\min}=(\mvis^{\rm leg}/\mtau)^{2}$ exceeds a typical fitted $x\simeq0.5$--$0.9$ only
once the leg mass passes $\sim1.26\,\si{\GeV}$, so below that the assigned mass is inert:
displacing the entire low-mass region by $200\,\si{\MeV}$ moves $\langle\mtautau\rangle$ by
$0.00\%$, against $-2.4\%$ for the same displacement in the $a_{1}$ tail. Replacing the
21-quantile grid with an explicit atom at $m_{\pi^{\pm}}$ plus a histogram CDF --- more
faithful to a distribution that genuinely has an atom --- changes $\langle\mtautau\rangle$
by $0.11\%$. \emph{(ii) A symmetric resolution mismatch.} Widening the response from $7\%$
to $25\%$ at fixed median moves the mean by $0.05\%$ (though it broadens the $\mtautau$
RMS by $\sim2\%$). Note the scope: that test used a roughly symmetric log-normal widening,
so it does \emph{not} cover the one-sided tail above. Of everything tested, only the energy
\emph{scale} --- linear in $\mtautau$ --- and a one-sided response tail remain capable of
what is observed.

\subsection{$\dR_{\tau\tau}$: a selection effect, cause unknown}
\label{sec:drtautau}

Comparing the \emph{generator-level} $\dR_{\tau\tau}$ and the selection efficiency against
it: the gen spectra agree, so the samples are the same physics and no generator-level
explanation is available. The efficiencies differ --- Delphes selects $0.22$--$0.23$ of
events at gen $\dR>2.5$ where CMS selects $0.15$--$0.17$ ($\sim$3$\sigma$), with a weaker
($\sim$2$\sigma$) hint that Delphes also loses pairs below $\dR<1$. Both push the
normalised distribution rightward. Three explanations were proposed and excluded by
measurement: fake $\tauh$ (the discrepancy lives entirely in the gen-matched component);
$\tau$-pair selection (making it fully symmetric moved $\dR_{\tau\tau}$ but not
$\mtautau$); and a large CMS $\tau$ energy miscalibration (the anchor response is
$0.97$--$0.99$).

This one matters more than its size suggests: it is a \emph{selection} effect, not a scale,
so no downstream correction can reach it. Unlike $\mtautau$, we have no candidate
mechanism.

\section{The decision to think about: keep correcting the jet, or rebuild the $\tauh$?}
\label{sec:decision}

A $\tauh$ in CMS is an HPS object built from a narrow set of decay products; in Delphes it
is an $R=0.4$ jet carrying \texttt{TauTag}. Its mass, energy and direction are therefore
all wrong at source, and we correct the first two against the anchor rather than fixing the
representation. The alternative is to rebuild it from the Delphes particle-flow candidates,
which the card already writes (the three \texttt{EFlow} collections --- tracks, photons,
neutral hadrons --- kept, per the card header, precisely for ``$\tau$ redefinition''):
seed from a jet, collect charged hadrons and photons in a narrow signal cone (fixed
$\dR\simeq0.1$, or $p_{T}$-dependent $3.0/p_{T}$ bounded at $0.05$--$0.1$), form the
standard decay-mode hypotheses, take the four-vector as the sum of signal-cone
constituents, and use an isolation annulus as a fake handle.

\paragraph{In favour.} Mass, energy and direction become correct \emph{by construction}
rather than by correction. For a phenomenological paper that removes a fast-simulation
limitation instead of documenting one. On cost, the implementation is contained and is
\emph{not} on the critical path --- running the full $5\times10^{5}$-event samples through
the ladder and training the NSBI is the multi-day item.

The measured tail of Section~\ref{sec:response} settles one thing: the \emph{current}
correction cannot work. A multiplicative per-$p_{T}$ median cannot depopulate a
kinematically forbidden region, and the Delphes response is one-sidedly broader at matched
median by a factor of three at low $p_{T}$. No refinement of \texttt{tau\_escale} as it
stands closes that gap.

That is narrower than ``only rebuilding can fix it'', and the distinction matters --- see
the third option below.

\paragraph{What does \emph{not} argue for it.} Rebuilding is sometimes said to retire
three corrections at once --- the visible-mass map, the energy scale and the visible-$\tau$
reference. That is badly weighted. The visible-mass map's residual imperfection is worth
$0.11\%$ on $\langle\mtautau\rangle$, so retiring it buys almost nothing: the map already
does the work. The visible-$\tau$ reference is a derivation-side simplification, not a
per-event gain. Effectively one of the three matters, and it is the energy scale.

\paragraph{A third option: correct the distribution, not the scale.} What the
measurement forbids is a \emph{multiplicative median} map, not every downstream
correction. For each gen-matched $\tau$-jet one could instead set
$p_{T}^{\rm reco}=p_{T}^{\rm gen}\times r$ with $r$ \emph{drawn} from the anchor's response
quantiles --- the machinery already used for the $\tauh$ visible mass, applied to the
energy. That reproduces the CMS response distribution including its tail, not merely its
median, and costs perhaps a day against weeks for a rebuild. Its drawbacks are real and
should be weighed rather than dismissed:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item it only applies to the $\sim$96\% gen-matched population; fakes have no gen $\tau$ to
scale from and would keep their jet $p_{T}$, as they do today;
\item $\ptmiss$ in Delphes is built from the reconstructed objects, so resampling the
$\tau$ $p_{T}$ afterwards breaks the balance. We already smear $\ptmiss$ independently, so
this is a difference of degree --- but it compounds;
\item the $\tau$ energy becomes a partly synthetic quantity. For an unbinned NSBI result
resting on realistic simulation, ``we resampled the $\tau$ energy response to match CMS''
is defensible but weaker than ``we rebuilt the $\tau$ from particle-flow candidates'' ---
again a difference of degree, since the visible mass is already sampled;
\item it does nothing for $\dR_{\tau\tau}$, which is a selection effect, and nothing for
decay mode or charge.
\end{itemize}
This option did not exist in earlier versions of this note and is not obviously wrong; it
is the cheapest thing that could address the measured defect, and the choice between it and
a rebuild is a judgement about what the paper should claim rather than about what closes
the $\mtautau$ gap.

\paragraph{Against, or at least uncertain.} It cannot reproduce DeepTau, so the
identification and fake-rate maps remain (they already work). It is \emph{not} obviously
the cure for $\dR_{\tau\tau}$: CMS HPS also seeds from AK4 jets, so the merging scales are
similar and a narrow signal cone may not change which pairs are resolved. And the current
treatment already agrees on eight of ten features and on the yield.

\paragraph{The caveat we most want checked.} Delphes neutral particle-flow candidates are
\emph{calorimeter towers} ($\sim0.02\times0.02$ in the barrel), not clustered PF objects.
Since $\pi^{0}$ content sets both the visible mass and the decay-mode assignment, an
HPS-like reconstruction on Delphes would be approximate in exactly the place that matters
most --- possibly no better than the anchor-sampled mass already in use.

\paragraph{Questions for $\tau$ expertise.}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item Is tower-granular neutral reconstruction adequate for a useful decay-mode
assignment, or does it degrade the $\pi^{0}$ content enough that the rebuilt mass is no
better than the anchor-sampled one?
\item Signal cone: fixed $0.1$ or $p_{T}$-dependent, over the $20$--$60\,\si{\GeV}$ range
that dominates here?
\item Seeding: jets (as CMS HPS does) or direct clustering of PF candidates? Would either
change the efficiency for collimated pairs, $\dR_{\tau\tau}\lesssim1$?
\item Is the $\dR_{\tau\tau}$ pattern --- over-efficient above $2.5$, possibly
under-efficient below $1$ --- recognisable as a jet-versus-HPS effect at all, or should we
look elsewhere?
\item Is a jet-based $\tauh$ with anchor-measured corrections defensible in a
phenomenological paper, or is rebuilding the expected standard?
\item Is a $1.18\times$ analysis-level yield excess acceptable for an unbinned NSBI study,
where training uses shapes and the normalisation is fitted?
\end{enumerate}

\appendix
\section{Reference: the studies behind these numbers}

All reproducible from the \texttt{delphes-pipeline} repository.

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{$\tautau$ cutflow} (\texttt{tauh\_cutflow.py}). Five stages --- gen visible
$\tauh$, reco object, ID, cleaned jets, FastMTT --- walked identically on both samples,
comparing \emph{conditional} efficiencies. Initially FastMTT alone carried the whole
deficit (Delphes retained $12\%$ against CMS's $99\%$); now $1.01$ overall. Unit tests
inject a loss at one known stage and assert it is attributed there and nowhere else.

\item \textbf{The FastMTT failure, and why the mass must be sampled.} The hadronic prior is
zero unless $x_{\min}=(\mvis/\mtau)^{2}\le1$; a Delphes $\tau$-jet carries the AK4 jet mass
($\mathcal{O}(5\text{--}20)\,\si{\GeV}$ against $\mtau=1.777$), so no solution exists and
$\mtautau$ returns NaN --- silently removing $\sim$89\% of pairs. Assigning the anchor
visible mass fixes it, but it must be \emph{sampled}, not averaged: the mass is a one-sided
floor, so a single median relaxes a real constraint for every leg above it, inflating the
width $\sim$60\% and the mean $\sim$9\% while leaving the \emph{mode} unchanged --- a
mode-gated $Z\rightarrow\tau\tau$ candle would not catch it.

\item \textbf{The reference error, twice.} A Delphes \texttt{GenJet} is also a jet and also
contains the underlying event, so it is \emph{not} the analogue of \texttt{GenVisTau}.
Against a genuine visible $\tau$ ($p_{\rm vis}=p_{\tau}-p_{\nu_\tau}$, exact since a
hadronic decay has one neutrino), the Delphes $\tau$-jet is $17\%$ harder at
$25\,\si{\GeV}$, falling to $\sim$2\% at $250\,\si{\GeV}$. The same error appeared
independently in the energy-scale map and in the cutflow's stage-1 acceptance.

\item \textbf{Order of application.} Maps measured against CMS-scale quantities must be
\emph{applied} at a CMS-scale $p_{T}$. Drawing tags on raw jets evaluated a steeply-rising
efficiency $\sim$17\% too far right; applying the energy scale first moved the
analysis-level excess $1.25\rightarrow1.18$.

\item \textbf{Gen-$\tau$ construction check} (\texttt{gen\_tau\_check.py}). Our
visible-$\tau$ construction scored against CMS's own \texttt{GenVisTau} on the same events:
efficiency $0.991$, purity $0.998$, reproducing CMS's stage-1 rate to $\sim$1.6\%.

\item \textbf{$\mvis$ and the gen-matched/fake split} (\texttt{nsbi\_overlay.py
--diagnostics --split-gen-matched}). $\mvis$ uses neither the $\ptmiss$ nor the fit, so it
attributes an $\mtautau$ offset to one of two disjoint causes; three unit tests pin the
properties the reading depends on ($\mvis\le\mtautau$ always; invariant under a
$40\rightarrow200\,\si{\GeV}$ $\ptmiss$ change; exactly linear in the $\tau$ energy scale).
The split normalises both components to the side's \emph{total}, so the area under the fake
curve is the fake fraction. Results in Section~\ref{sec:mtautau}.

\item \textbf{$\tau$ energy response shape} (\texttt{tau\_response.py}). The response as
a distribution, per gen-$p_{T}$ bin, with a lower row in which each side is divided by its
own median --- so the upper row shows what \texttt{tau\_escale} is built to remove and the
lower row shows what it cannot. Unit tests assert exactly that separation: a pure scale
difference vanishes under median normalisation, while an injected one-sided tail survives
it. Two flags expose an asymmetry in the shipped derivation --- the anchor gates its
acceptance on the \emph{gen} visible $\tau$ while the Delphes side gates on the \emph{reco}
jet (\texttt{--gate}), and the anchor always conditions on DeepTau while the Delphes side
conditions on nothing (\texttt{--require-id}). Result in Section~\ref{sec:response}.

\item \textbf{Map accuracy audit.} No map is statistics-limited at the configured
$2\times10^{5}$ anchor events: $b$-tag $0.25$--$0.42\%$ per bin, $\tauh$ efficiency
$0.43$--$1.26\%$, energy scale $0.2$--$0.3\%$, $\ptmiss$ resolution $0.16$--$0.21\%$. What
limits a map is the numerator $np$, not $n$. Two structural defects do not improve with
statistics --- the mass quantile grid interpolates across the physically empty region
between the $\pi^{\pm}$ atom and the $\rho$ shoulder, over-populating it $\sim6\times$; and
the $300\,\si{\GeV}$ flat extrapolation --- but the first was measured inert and the second
is untested.

\item \textbf{Generator copies.} Three of our own errors had one cause: Delphes writes the
full Pythia history, so each physical $\tau$ appears several times, while CMS
\texttt{GenPart} is pruned. The rule: anything that \textbf{counts} generator objects must
collapse copies first; anything that \textbf{matches} them geometrically need not, since
copies are collinear.
\end{enumerate}
