\title{\vspace{-1.5cm}A $\tautau$ cutflow diagnostic for the Delphes
HH$\rightarrow b\bar{b}\tau\tau$ sample:\\
locating a factor $\sim$5 yield deficit against CMS NanoAOD}
\author{Jingjing Pan}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The tuned Delphes HH$\rightarrow b\bar b\tau^{+}\tau^{-}$ sample selects
$\sim$5$\times$ fewer $\tautau$ events than the CMS NanoAOD reference from the same
number of generated events. A five-stage cutflow, applied identically to both
samples, localises the entire deficit to a \emph{single} stage: the FastMTT di-$\tau$
mass reconstruction, which keeps $0.111$ of Delphes events against $0.994$ of CMS
events. Up to that stage Delphes is in fact \emph{ahead} of CMS in cumulative
efficiency. The cause is identified as a definitional mismatch rather than a detector
or tuning effect: in Delphes a $\tauh$ \emph{is} a jet and therefore carries the AK4
jet mass ($\mathcal{O}(5\text{--}20)\,\si{\GeV}$), whereas the FastMTT hadronic decay
prior vanishes identically unless $\mvis \le \mtau = 1.777\,\si{\GeV}$. Roughly 89\%
of Delphes $\tautau$ pairs therefore admit no solution and return NaN. A proposed fix
--- an anchor-measured $\tauh$ visible-mass tuning map --- is described. This note is
circulated for cross-check before the correction is adopted.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation}

The Delphes fast-simulation sample is intended to reproduce the CMS
HH$\rightarrow b\bar b\tau\tau$ Run-3 baseline closely enough to support a
phenomenological unbinned NSBI $\kl$ measurement. Object-level agreement is already
established: the tuned b-tagging, $\tauh$ efficiency, energy scales and lepton scale
factors are anchored to a private 2024 NanoAODv15 sample, and the Level-0/1/4
validation gates pass. The $m_{HH}$ and $m_{bb}$ distributions --- the $\kl$-carrying
observables --- agree with CMS within statistics.

One discrepancy resisted every object-level fix: from $2\times10^{4}$ generated events
the overlay selects $\approx$300 $\tautau$ events on Delphes against $\approx$1500 on
CMS. Because this ratio directly caps the statistics available for NSBI training, it
matters \emph{which} stage of the selection creates it. A per-stage efficiency
comparison is the minimal measurement that answers this.

\section{Method}

Both samples are walked through the same five stages. Definitions are matched
object-for-object; where the two data tiers expose different collections, the
counterpart with the same physical meaning is used.

Two choices deserve comment. First, stage 1 uses Delphes \texttt{GenJet}s rather than
the gen $\tau$ four-vector as the visible-$\tau$ proxy: \texttt{GenJet}s are clustered
after the neutrino filter and are therefore already the visible object, matching the
definition of \texttt{GenVisTau}. Binning the Delphes side by the \emph{full} gen-$\tau$
$p_{T}$ would compare different quantities, since a hadronic $\tau$ carries only
$\sim$65\% of its momentum visibly. Second, stage 4 applies a symmetric overlap
removal: the di-$\tau$ pair is selected first, then jets within $\dR<0.4$ of either
selected $\tau$ are removed on \emph{both} sides. Without this, the Delphes side
removes $\tau$-tagged jets from the $b$ pool while the CMS side does not (there
$\tauh$ live in a separate \texttt{Tau} collection but their jets remain in
\texttt{Jet}), which previously produced a spurious $\dR_{bb}$ discrepancy.

\paragraph{Reading the tables.}
Stages 3 and 4 agree between the two samples to within a few percent at all three
$\kl$ points ($\Delta\varepsilon = 0.96$--$1.09$ and $0.98$--$1.00$): the $\tauh$
identification and the jet selection are \emph{not} the problem. Stage~5 is
catastrophic and consistent across $\kl$: Delphes retains $11$--$12\%$ where CMS
retains $99\%$. Crucially, the cumulative efficiency through stage~4 is
\emph{higher} on Delphes ($0.098$ vs $0.063$ at $\kl=1$); the entire deficit, and more,
is generated at stage~5 alone.

\paragraph{Known artefacts in stages 1--2.}
The stage-1 ratio $\Delta\varepsilon\simeq2.1$ is an artefact of the diagnostic itself,
not of the simulation. The Delphes \texttt{GenJet} proxy cannot distinguish a hadronic
from a leptonic $\tau$ decay --- a $\tau\rightarrow\ell\nu\bar\nu$ also produces a
\texttt{GenJet} --- so the Delphes denominator is inflated by roughly
$1/\mathcal{B}(\tautau)$. Stage 2 ($\Delta\varepsilon\simeq0.75$) is the mirror image:
those leptonic-$\tau$ events fail to produce a $\tau$-jet. The two largely cancel and
neither contributes to the final deficit. Removing this artefact requires the gen
decay chain, which the current reader does not retain.

\section{Cause}

In Delphes a $\tauh$ candidate \emph{is} a jet, so the object handed to the di-$\tau$
mass estimator carries the AK4 jet mass --- underlying event and additional particles
clustered within $R=0.4$ --- typically $5$--$20\,\si{\GeV}$. A reconstructed CMS
$\tauh$ instead carries its decay-mode visible mass, bounded above by
$\mtau=1.777\,\si{\GeV}$ ($\pi^{\pm}$: $0.14$; $\rho$: $0.77$; $a_{1}$: $1.26\,
\si{\GeV}$).

The covariance-free FastMTT estimator maximises, per event, a likelihood over the
visible energy fractions $x_{1},x_{2}\in(0,1]$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(x_1,x_2) \;=\;
\mathcal{L}_{\rm MET}(x_1,x_2)\;\prod_{i=1,2}\mathcal{L}_{\rm dec}(x_i)\,,
\qquad
\mtautau \;=\; \frac{\mvis}{\sqrt{x_1x_2}}\,,
\end{equation}
where the hadronic decay prior is the phase-space density
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\rm dec}(x) \;=\;
\begin{cases}
1, & x_{\min}\le x\le 1\,,\\[2pt]
0, & \text{otherwise}\,,
\end{cases}
\qquad
x_{\min}\;\equiv\;\left(\frac{\mvis}{\mtau}\right)^{2}\,.
\label{eq:prior}
\end{equation}
If $\mvis>\mtau$ then $x_{\min}>1$ and the admissible interval in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:prior} is \emph{empty}: the prior vanishes for every $x$, the likelihood
is identically zero, and the estimator returns NaN. Table~\ref{tab:xmin} shows the
effect on a $40$-point scan.

The $11\%$ of Delphes events that do survive are those rare pairs in which
\emph{both} $\tau$-jets happen to have a mass below $\mtau$ --- a biased, unusually
narrow subset. This also offers a natural explanation for two residual shape
discrepancies seen in the feature overlays, namely a $\mtautau$ peak shifted
$\sim$$10\,\si{\GeV}$ high and a $\dR_{\tau\tau}$ distribution displaced to larger
values, and for the low acceptance$\times$efficiency ($0.082$) reported by the
Level-4 $m_{HH}$ gate.

\section{Proposed correction}

The Delphes $\tauh$ four-vector should carry a physical $\tau$ visible mass. The jet
mass is a clustering artefact, not a property of the $\tau$, and since
$\mtautau=\mvis/\sqrt{x_1x_2}$ it sets the $\mtautau$ \emph{scale} as well as
determining whether a solution exists at all.

The correction adopted follows the existing tuning pattern used for the energy scales:
the median $\tauh$ visible mass is measured on the CMS anchor as a function of $p_{T}$
(from \texttt{Tau} objects passing DeepTau Medium), stored as a tuning map, and applied
downstream to $\tau$-tagged Delphes jets. It requires no change to the Delphes card and
no re-production: the existing samples are corrected in place at analysis level. The
implementation applies the map after the energy-scale correction (so the lookup uses
the corrected $p_{T}$ and the assigned mass is not subsequently rescaled), caps the
assigned mass strictly below $\mtau$, and leaves all non-$\tau$ jets untouched, so
$m_{bb}$ and the $b$-side observables are unaffected.

\paragraph{Why the mass must be sampled, not averaged.}
An initial implementation assigned the per-$p_{T}$ \emph{median} anchor mass
deterministically, on the expectation that losing the decay-mode spread would affect the
$\mtautau$ resolution but not its scale. That expectation is wrong, and the reason is
worth recording because it is easy to get backwards. The visible mass does not enter
FastMTT as a smearing: via Eq.~\eqref{eq:prior} it is a \emph{one-sided lower bound} on
$x$. Its contribution to the numerator $\mvis$ of $\mtautau=\mvis/\sqrt{x_1x_2}$ is
negligible ($m^{2}\sim1.6\,\si{\GeV\squared}$ against
$\mvis^{2}\sim3600\,\si{\GeV\squared}$). Assigning a single value therefore
\emph{relaxes} a real constraint for every leg whose true mass lies above it, opening the
low-$x$ region in which $\mtautau$ diverges.

The effect is large and one-sided. For a 3-prong $a_1$ leg
($\mvis=1.26\,\si{\GeV}$, true floor $x_{\min}=0.503$) given the median
$0.77\,\si{\GeV}$ ($x_{\min}=0.188$), with $p_T^{\rm miss}$ favouring $x\simeq0.25$, the
fit moves from $x=0.525$ to $x=0.250$ and $\mtautau$ from $224$ to
$470\,\si{\GeV}$ --- a factor $2.1$. Over a realistic decay-mode mixture the median
assignment inflates the $\mtautau$ width by ${\sim}60\%$ and pulls the mean up by
${\sim}9\%$, with the upward tail growing accordingly, while leaving the \emph{mode}
unchanged. Two consequences deserve emphasis: the $Z\rightarrow\tau\tau$ candle gates the
mode and would not have caught it, and because the distortion is applied to the Delphes
side only it does \emph{not} cancel in the CMS comparison --- unlike the covariance-free
MET approximation, which both sides share.

The implementation therefore stores the anchor's per-$p_{T}$ mass \emph{quantiles} and
draws a mass per $\tau$-jet from them, using the random stream already threaded through
the re-tagging so the tuning lens and the ntuplizer remain bit-identical. Resampling from
the correct marginal restores the $\mtautau$ mean to within ${\sim}0.5\%$ and the width to
within ${\sim}2\%$ of the true per-decay-mode result. \emph{A residual limitation
remains}: the mass is drawn independently of the $\tau$ energy and of the partner leg,
whereas the true visible mass correlates with the decay mode and hence with the
prong multiplicity and the energy sharing.

\section{Points for cross-check}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Is the diagnosis accepted?} The claim is that a single definitional
mismatch --- jet mass versus $\tau$ visible mass --- accounts for the entire
$\tautau$ yield deficit, and that no detector-level or tuning effect need be invoked.
\item \textbf{Is quantile sampling of the visible mass sufficient?} The spread is now
reproduced, but the drawn mass is independent of the $\tau$ energy and of the partner
leg. Would sampling a decay mode per $\tau$ (and deriving the mass from it) be preferable,
so that the mass, the prong multiplicity and the energy sharing stay correlated?
\item \textbf{Should the $\tauh$ four-momentum be corrected further?} Only the mass is
addressed here; the $\tau$-jet direction and energy already receive an energy-scale
correction, but the jet axis may differ from the HPS $\tauh$ axis.
\item \textbf{Stage-1/2 artefact.} Is it worth retaining the gen decay chain in the
reader so hadronic and leptonic $\tau$ decays can be separated, making stages 1--2
directly comparable?
\item \textbf{Residual shape differences.} If the correction does not fully resolve the
$\mtautau$ and $\dR_{\tau\tau}$ shifts, the next candidate is the MET resolution, which
the tuning lens still reports as requiring adjustment (Delphes MET is
\emph{cleaner} than the CMS anchor) and which enters FastMTT directly.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Reproducing}

The diagnostic is \texttt{scripts/tauh\_cutflow.py} in the \texttt{delphes-pipeline}
repository:
\begin{verbatim}
python scripts/tauh_cutflow.py --config config.v1.yml \
    --delphes-dir /ceph/jpan/gen-delphes \
    --nano-dir    /ceph/jpan/cms_nanoaod_2024_hh2b2tau \
    --max-events 20000 --out outputs/validation_v1/tauh_cutflow.md
\end{verbatim}
Unit tests inject a loss at one known stage at a time and assert the cutflow
attributes it to that stage and no other.
