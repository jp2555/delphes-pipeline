\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The tuned Delphes HH$\rightarrow b\bar b\tau^{+}\tau^{-}$ sample initially selected
$\sim$5$\times$ \emph{fewer} $\tautau$ events than the CMS NanoAOD reference, and its
$\mtautau$ peaked $\sim$25\,\si{\GeV} high. A five-stage cutflow localised the yield
deficit to a single stage --- the FastMTT di-$\tau$ mass --- and the cause turned out
not to be a detector or tuning effect but a representational one: \textbf{in Delphes a
$\tauh$ \emph{is} an AK4 jet, whereas in CMS it is an HPS $\tauh$ object}. Nearly every
discrepancy we found traces to that one difference, expressed in a different component
of the four-vector or in a different reference used to tune it. Six corrections are
described, each anchored to the CMS NanoAOD and applied downstream so that no Delphes
re-production is required. The $\tautau$ yield ratio moved from $0.20$ to $1.25$ and the
$\mtautau$ peak offset from $\sim$25 to $\sim$7\,\si{\GeV}; seven of the ten NSBI input
features now agree with CMS within statistics. Two discrepancies remain open. This note
is circulated for cross-check.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation and summary}

The Delphes fast-simulation sample must reproduce the CMS HH$\rightarrow b\bar
b\tau\tau$ Run-3 baseline closely enough to support a phenomenological unbinned NSBI
$\kl$ measurement. The $b\bar b$ side and the $\kl$-carrying observable $m_{HH}$ agreed
from early on; the $\tau\tau$ side did not, in two ways that resisted object-level
tuning: a factor $\sim$5 yield deficit and a $\sim$25\,\si{\GeV} shift in $\mtautau$.

Table~\ref{tab:progress} is the summary. Each row is one correction, all of them
downstream of the Delphes card (the production is unchanged), and each was adopted only
after a measurement identified it.

\section{One root cause, several components}

A CMS $\tauh$ is an HPS object built from a narrow set of decay products. A Delphes
$\tauh$ is an anti-$k_{T}$ $R=0.4$ jet that happens to carry \texttt{TauTag}. The two
therefore differ in \emph{every} component of the four-vector, and additionally in the
generator-level reference one would naturally use to tune each component:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{mass} --- the jet mass is $\mathcal{O}(5\text{--}20)\,\si{\GeV}$; a $\tauh$
visible mass is bounded by $\mtau=1.777\,\si{\GeV}$ (correction~1);
\item \textbf{energy} --- the jet carries the underlying event inside the cone, so it is
$\sim$17\% harder than the visible $\tau$ at low $p_{T}$ (corrections~4 and~6);
\item \textbf{reference} --- a Delphes \texttt{GenJet} is also a jet and also contains
the underlying event, so it is \emph{not} the analogue of \texttt{GenVisTau}
(correction~6);
\item \textbf{identity} --- ``matched to a gen $\tau$'' does not mean ``hadronic''
(correction~2), and the two data tiers expose $\tau$ candidates with different intrinsic
thresholds (correction~3).
\end{itemize}

Only correction~5 ($\ptmiss$) is unrelated to this; it follows from the card running
without pileup by design.

\subsection{Correction 1: the $\tauh$ visible mass, and why it must be sampled}

The covariance-free FastMTT estimator maximises, per event, a likelihood over the
visible energy fractions $x_{1},x_{2}\in(0,1]$, with
$\mtautau = \mvis/\sqrt{x_1x_2}$ and a hadronic decay prior
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\rm dec}(x) =
\begin{cases} 1, & x_{\min}\le x\le 1,\\ 0, & \text{otherwise,}\end{cases}
\qquad
x_{\min}\equiv\left(\mvis/\mtau\right)^{2}.
\label{eq:prior}
\end{equation}
If $\mvis>\mtau$ then $x_{\min}>1$, the admissible interval is empty, the likelihood
vanishes identically and the estimator returns NaN. A Delphes $\tau$-jet carrying its
jet mass therefore admits \emph{no solution at all}: $\sim$89\% of $\tautau$ pairs were
silently lost, which was the whole of the stage-5 collapse.

The correction measures the CMS $\tauh$ visible mass on the anchor and assigns it to
$\tau$-tagged Delphes jets. Crucially it must be \textbf{sampled, not averaged}. An
initial implementation assigned the per-$p_{T}$ median; that is wrong because via
Eq.~\eqref{eq:prior} the mass is a one-sided \emph{lower bound} on $x$, not a smearing.
Its contribution to the numerator $\mvis$ is negligible
($m^{2}\sim1.6$ against $\mvis^{2}\sim3600\,\si{\GeV\squared}$), so a single value
\emph{relaxes} a real constraint for every leg whose true mass lies above it. For a
3-prong $a_1$ leg ($\mvis=1.26$, floor $x_{\min}=0.503$) given the median $0.77$
($x_{\min}=0.188$), with $\ptmiss$ favouring $x\simeq0.25$, the fit moves from
$x=0.525$ to $x=0.250$ and $\mtautau$ from $224$ to $470\,\si{\GeV}$. Over a realistic
decay-mode mixture the median assignment inflates the $\mtautau$ width by $\sim$60\% and
the mean by $\sim$9\% while leaving the \emph{mode} unchanged --- so the
$Z\rightarrow\tau\tau$ candle, which gates the mode, would not have caught it. The
implementation therefore stores the anchor's per-$p_{T}$ mass \emph{quantiles} and draws
per $\tau$-jet from them.

\subsection{Corrections 2--3: identity and acceptance}

The $\tauh$ efficiency map is measured on the anchor as \texttt{GenVisTau}$\rightarrow$
DeepTau-Medium \texttt{Tau}, and \texttt{GenVisTau} exists only for hadronic decays.
Treating every gen $\tau$ as genuine handed a leptonic $\tau$'s jet that efficiency
($\sim$0.5) instead of the jet$\rightarrow\tauh$ fake rate ($\sim$0.004), fabricating
$\tautau$ events out of $\tau_h\tau_\ell$ and $\tau_\ell\tau_\ell$ ones. A gen $\tau$ is
now classified by looking for a status-1 $e/\mu$ whose first non-self-copy ancestor is a
$\tau$, using the generator mother links only.

Separately, the two tiers expose $\tau$ candidates with different intrinsic floors: a
Delphes $\tauh$ is a jet and so exists down to the card's \texttt{JetPTMin} of
$15\,\si{\GeV}$ and out to $|\eta|<2.5$, while the CMS \texttt{Tau} collection begins
near $18$--$20\,\si{\GeV}$. A common acceptance ($p_{T}>20$, $|\eta|<2.3$, the analysis
values) is now applied to both sides before the pair is chosen.

\subsection{Corrections 4 and 6: the $\tau$ energy scale and its reference}

The energy-scale map was originally derived by inverting the Delphes response to unity
against its own \texttt{GenJet} --- a \emph{self}-comparison, where every other map in
the framework is a comparison \emph{to CMS}. Correction~4 replaced it with the anchor
ratio,
$\texttt{tau\_escale} = \mathcal{R}_{\rm CMS}/\mathcal{R}_{\rm Delphes}$, in the same
form already used for the lepton scale factors.

That alone changed almost nothing (1--3\%), and the reason is instructive: the two
responses were still measured against \emph{different} references. The CMS response is
$\texttt{Tau}/\texttt{GenVisTau}$ --- clean --- while the Delphes response was
$\tau\text{-jet}/\texttt{GenJet}$, and a \texttt{GenJet} is an $R=0.4$ cluster that
still contains the underlying event (only \emph{pileup} is absent from the card).
Correction~6 builds a genuine visible gen $\tau$ on the Delphes side using the identity
that a hadronic $\tau$ decay has exactly one neutrino,
\begin{equation}
p_{\rm vis} = p_{\tau} - p_{\nu_\tau},
\end{equation}
with the $\nu_\tau$ located through the same mother-link walk (no generator daughter
links are needed). Profiled against \emph{that}, the Delphes $\tau$-jet is
\textbf{17\% harder than the visible $\tau$ at $25\,\si{\GeV}$}, falling to $\sim$2\% at
$250\,\si{\GeV}$ --- the shape expected of a roughly fixed underlying-event offset. The
resulting map is $\texttt{tau\_escale} = [0.85, 0.88, 0.90, 0.91, 0.93, 0.94, 0.94,
0.95]$, against $[0.91,\ldots,0.96]$ before.

\subsection{Correction 5: $\ptmiss$ resolution (pileup)}

The card runs without pileup by construction, and its header states that ``pileup enters
through the tuning maps''. That map had never been built. Measured with the same
estimator on both sides, the per-component $\ptmiss$ resolution is
$16.5\,\si{\GeV}$ on Delphes against $32.6\,\si{\GeV}$ on the CMS anchor --- a factor of
two, and the largest single mismatch anywhere in the map set. Gaussian noise of the
quadrature difference ($28.1\,\si{\GeV}$) is added per component. Since $\ptmiss$ is what
FastMTT fits the energy fractions against, this is the correction that moved $\mtautau$
most: the peak offset fell from $\sim$25 to $\sim$12\,\si{\GeV} and the distribution
broadened from far-too-narrow into the right range.

\section{What is still open}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{$\dR_{\tau\tau}$ shoulder.} Delphes carries an excess at
$\dR_{\tau\tau}\simeq2.2$--$3.2$ that CMS does not. It has survived every correction
above, which is informative: none of them touched the $\tau$ \emph{direction} or the
selected \emph{population}. Large $\dR_{\tau\tau}$ corresponds to a low-$p_{T}$ Higgs,
hence soft $\tau$.
\item \textbf{Residual yield excess} of $1.25\times$ and a $\mtautau$ distribution still
somewhat broader than CMS.
\end{enumerate}

A single candidate explains both, and is our current working hypothesis. The downstream
corrections are applied in the order (b-tag, $\tauh$ tag, energy scale, mass), so the
$\tauh$ efficiency is drawn at the \emph{raw} jet $p_{T}$ --- which correction~6 shows to
be $\sim$17\% too high at low $p_{T}$. The efficiency rises steeply there
($0.39\rightarrow0.52$ between 25 and $35\,\si{\GeV}$), so evaluating it $\sim$17\% too
far to the right produces a systematic over-efficiency for soft $\tau$: too many
reconstructed pairs, concentrated exactly in the low-$p_{T}$, large-$\dR_{\tau\tau}$
population. Applying the energy scale \emph{first} would make every subsequent map
evaluate at a CMS-like $p_{T}$. This is falsifiable: if the hypothesis is right the
excess should vanish preferentially at low $\tau$ $p_{T}$ and take the shoulder with it;
if the shoulder survives a corrected low-$p_{T}$ efficiency it is genuinely angular, and
the $\tau$ direction (jet axis versus HPS axis) is the next candidate.

\paragraph{Hypotheses tested and rejected.} For the record, three explanations for the
$\mtautau$ shift were proposed and excluded by direct measurement: (i) \emph{fake
$\tauh$} --- the gen-matched/fake split gives fake fractions of 5.7\% (Delphes) against
5.0\% (CMS), essentially equal, with both shape discrepancies living entirely in the
gen-matched component; (ii) \emph{$\tau$-pair selection} --- making the selection fully
symmetric moved $\dR_{\tau\tau}$ but left $\mtautau$ untouched; (iii) \emph{a large CMS
$\tau$ energy miscalibration} --- the anchor response is $0.97$--$0.99$, so CMS $\tauh$
are calibrated to the visible $\tau$ to within a few percent.

\paragraph{Not addressed here.} Pileup also affects the jet energy \emph{resolution}
(only the scale is corrected) and produces pileup jets that Delphes cannot make at all.
Both act on the $b\bar b$ side, which currently agrees well with CMS, and the second
cannot be fixed downstream in any case.

\section{Points for cross-check}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Is the framing accepted?} The claim is that essentially the whole
$\tau$-side discrepancy follows from representing a $\tauh$ as an AK4 jet, and that the
remedy is to correct each component of that four-vector against a CMS-anchored,
like-for-like reference.
\item \textbf{Is quantile sampling of the visible mass sufficient?} The spread is
reproduced, but the drawn mass is independent of the $\tau$ energy and of the partner
leg. Would sampling a decay mode per $\tau$ be preferable, so that mass, prong
multiplicity and energy sharing stay correlated?
\item \textbf{Is isotropic $\ptmiss$ smearing adequate?} Real $\ptmiss$ resolution is
anisotropic and scales with $\Sigma E_{T}$; ours is round and flat. Delphes $\mtautau$ is
now marginally \emph{broader} than CMS, which is what one would expect from
over-simplified noise.
\item \textbf{The order-of-application hypothesis} in Section~5 --- does it look right,
and is applying the energy scale first the correct remedy?
\item \textbf{Residual normalisation.} Is a $1.25\times$ yield excess acceptable for an
unbinned NSBI study, given that the training uses shapes and the normalisation is
fitted, or should it be closed before production?
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Reproducing}

All corrections live in \texttt{delphes-pipeline} and are applied downstream of the
Delphes card; the existing production is unchanged. Re-derive the maps and remake the
comparison with
\begin{verbatim}
python -m delphes_pipeline.tuning.derive_maps --config config.v1.yml \
    --output cards/tuning/maps_v1.json
python scripts/tauh_cutflow.py  --config config.v1.yml [...]  # the diagnostic
python scripts/nsbi_overlay.py  --config config.v1.yml [...] \
    --tautau-only --diagnostics
\end{verbatim}
The cutflow's unit tests inject a loss at one known stage at a time and assert it is
attributed to that stage and no other; the overlay's tests assert that the same physical
object is accepted or rejected identically on both sides.
