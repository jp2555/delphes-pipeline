\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The $\tautau$ yield deficit that motivated this work (a factor $\sim$5) is closed: the
cutflow agrees with CMS at every stage ($1.01$) and eight of ten NSBI features agree. The
two that do not, $\mtautau$ and $\dR_{\tau\tau}$, are both on the $\tau$ side. The
$\mtautau$ offset is now traced: it is in the $\tau$ four-vectors, fakes are excluded, and
the Delphes $\tau$ energy response carries a one-sided tail CMS does not have --- at gen
$p_{T}=20$--$30\,\si{\GeV}$, one Delphes $\tau$ in ten has more than twice the median
response, against $1.08$ on CMS. A multiplicative median map cannot reshape a distribution,
so the current correction cannot close this. That leaves three options, priced in
Section~\ref{sec:decision}; the measurements are in Section~\ref{sec:open}.
\end{abstract}

\section{The decision}
\label{sec:decision}

A CMS $\tauh$ is an HPS object built from a narrow set of decay products; a Delphes $\tauh$
is an $R=0.4$ jet carrying \texttt{TauTag}. Mass, energy and direction are therefore wrong
at source and we correct the first two rather than fix the representation.
Section~\ref{sec:mtautau} shows the correction cannot succeed as built: a multiplicative
per-$p_{T}$ median cannot depopulate a kinematically forbidden region, and the Delphes
response is one-sidedly broader at matched median by $3\times$ at low $p_{T}$. That is
narrower than ``only rebuilding works'' --- hence three options, not two.

The three options have now been costed and their accuracy quantified: throughput and
storage by benchmark, the accuracy axis by a toy that propagates the \emph{measured}
response quantiles (Table~\ref{tab:response}) into $\mtautau$ for $H(125)$, $Z(91)$ and a
$t\bar t$ continuum. Scale context: the full campaign is $\sim$300M events (3$\times$5M
signal, 3$\times$50M $t\bar t$, $\sim$100M DY), and the fake-$\tauh$ population inverts
there --- in the signal selection fakes are 4\% and demonstrably irrelevant, but in
\texttt{TTto4Q} every selected $\tauh$ is a fake by construction, and fakes currently keep
their raw jet $p_{T}$.

\paragraph{(A) Rebuild the $\tauh$ from particle flow.} The samples retain the EFlow
candidates (32\,TB), so A needs no re-production; the cone kernel benchmarks 5--20$\times$
above the pipeline wall and the added read volume is $\sim$13\%, so A's cost is purely
3--5 weeks of development and 10--25 tuning iterations (cone size, strip merging,
$\pi^{0}$ threshold). Its risks are now itemised: 60--70\% likelihood that tower-granular
neutrals ($0.017\times0.017$, but with smeared centres) make the rebuilt \emph{mass} no
better than the sampled one, and 50--60\% that a residual energy-scale map returns anyway
(a narrow cone removes contamination, but Delphes calorimeter thresholds still differ from
CMS PF). Its unique, B-inaccessible deliverable is the $\tau$ \emph{direction} --- the one
lever on $\dR_{\tau\tau}$, which no energy correction reaches.

\paragraph{(B) Correct the distribution, not the scale.} For each gen-matched $\tau$-jet
set $p_{T}^{\rm reco}=p_{T}^{\rm gen}\times r$ with $r$ drawn from the CMS response
quantiles --- the machinery already used for the visible mass. By construction the
gen-matched response then \emph{is} the CMS response: 100\% fidelity, exactly. The
measured marginal cost is 0.02\% of pipeline CPU. Three extensions close its gaps:
a \emph{fake-response map} (CMS fake response measured on the $t\bar t$ anchor already on
disk: gen-unmatched \texttt{Tau} vs matched \texttt{GenJet}) recovers the fake-energy
mismodeling in full; \emph{vectorial MET propagation} (subtract the old $\tau$ vectors,
add the new) closes the balance objection; and one \emph{transfer check} of the CMS
response quantiles between the HH and $t\bar t$ anchors (medians within 1\%, quantile
ratios within 5\%) validates using one map across processes --- if DY is doubted, a 200k-event
DY anchor is a $\sim$2--3\,GB download. What B leaves untouched: the $\tau$ direction,
decay mode, and charge.

\paragraph{(C) Accept and document --- now excluded by measurement.} With medians
perfectly aligned, the residual \emph{shape} alone widens $H$ by 21\% and $Z$ by 33\%,
drops the $H$-vs-$Z$ AUC from 0.976 to 0.905, and multiplies $Z\rightarrow\tau\tau$
leakage into the $H$ window \textbf{by 5.2} (2.5\%$\rightarrow$13.3\%). A 1D Fisher fit
puts C at 90\% (84--92) of the CMS $\mtautau$ sensitivity. The DY leakage would be visible
in the paper's own $\mtautau$ figure --- a reviewer problem independent of the $\kl$
number.

\paragraph{Reading the fidelity numbers.} They are 1D bounds on the $\mtautau$-mediated
share of the $\kl$ error, not the $\kl$ error itself ($\kl$ information lives mostly in
$m_{HH}$, identical across options). Two further caveats: the response's \emph{lower} half
is unmeasured (all scenarios share a mirrored CMS-like lower tail), and A's 96\% assumes a
30\% core widening from tower granularity --- a labelled guess. The fake near-cancellation
in the 1D fit (0.5--1.7\%) is toy-specific; a 10-feature NSBI sees the full $+16$\,GeV /
20\%-width fake-shape mismodeling (13\% of the dominant background's shape
non-overlapping), which is why the fake map is not optional for B.

\paragraph{Cost is not the axis.} The full 300M-event ntuplization is $\sim$185 core-hours
flat, and $\sim$50 with the (verified bit-identical) restricted-ancestry walk; DY events
are $\sim$10$\times$ lighter still. Storage: the ntuplizer currently writes the full gen
record --- 99.5\% of a 9\,TB output; pruning to $\tau$ ancestry $+$ bosons cuts it to
$\sim$0.4\,TB. Both worth doing under any option. The real trade is \emph{weeks of
development and tuning judgement (A) versus a day (B)}, against the accuracy column above.

\paragraph{Recommendation.} \textbf{B$+$ now} (resampling, fake map, MET propagation,
anchor transfer check): $<$1\% $\mtautau$ fidelity error for the whole campaign, this
month. \textbf{Not C.} \textbf{Gate A on the one thing only it can fix}: measure the
$\tau$ \emph{axis} residual (jet axis vs gen-visible-$\tau$ axis, both tiers --- the same
probe pattern as the response study, angle instead of energy; a few hours on existing
files). If Delphes' axis residual is large where CMS's is small, A has a unique
quantified justification for $\dR_{\tau\tau}$ and the weeks become defensible; if not,
B$+$ closes the case. Keep the 32\,TB of EFlow until that measurement is done.

\paragraph{Questions for $\tau$ expertise.}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0pt
\item Is tower-granular neutral reconstruction adequate for a useful decay-mode assignment,
or does it degrade $\pi^{0}$ content enough that the rebuilt mass is no better than the
anchor-sampled one?
\item Signal cone: fixed $0.1$ or $p_{T}$-dependent, over the $20$--$60\,\si{\GeV}$ range
that dominates here?
\item Seeding: jets (as HPS) or direct PF clustering? Would either change the efficiency
for collimated pairs, $\dR_{\tau\tau}\lesssim1$?
\item Is the $\dR_{\tau\tau}$ pattern --- over-efficient above $2.5$, possibly
under-efficient below $1$ --- recognisable as a jet-versus-HPS effect?
\item Is option (B) acceptable in a pheno paper, or does resampling a reconstructed
quantity undercut the claim?
\item Is a $1.18\times$ analysis-level yield excess acceptable for unbinned NSBI, where
training uses shapes and normalisation is fitted?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Known limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

In four buckets, because ``known'' was covering
different things.

\emph{Accepted by design.} FastMTT is covariance-free (a fixed $\ptmiss$ resolution rather
than the per-event covariance), but is run identically on both sides, so it cancels in the
comparison and bounds the absolute $\mtautau$ resolution rather than the agreement. The card
runs without pileup, so jet energy \emph{resolution} is untuned and pileup jets cannot
exist; both act on the $b\bar b$ side, which agrees, and the second cannot be fixed
downstream in any case. $\ptmiss$ smearing is isotropic and flat where the real resolution
is anisotropic and grows with $\Sigma E_{T}$: this reproduces the width but not its
structure, and it remains a candidate for $\mtautau$ being marginally \emph{broader} on
Delphes --- Section~\ref{sec:mtautau} exonerates $\ptmiss$ for the \emph{offset}, not for
the width.

\emph{A scope bound, not a defect.} \texttt{tau\_mistag} is measured on the $b$-rich signal
anchor, and fake rates are strongly jet-flavour dependent, so it is \emph{not} valid for the
fake-dominated backgrounds (QCD, W+jets). This does not limit the present signal study, but
the samples cannot support a background-heavy analysis until those are measured separately.

\emph{Real defects, not yet fixed.} Two in the energy-scale \emph{application}: fake $\tauh$
match neither branch of \texttt{escale\_factor} and are left uncorrected while still
receiving a $\tau$-like mass, and the $\tau$ branch matches \emph{all} gen $\tau$
(\texttt{|pid|==15}) including leptonically decaying ones, whereas the map is derived on the
hadronic visible set. Both were measured \emph{not} to be the cause of the $\mtautau$ offset
(Section~\ref{sec:mtautau}), but both are wrong.

\emph{Untested --- risks, not accepted limitations.} \textbf{(a)} The sampled $\tauh$ mass
is drawn independently of the $\tau$ energy and of the partner leg, whereas the true visible
mass correlates with decay mode and hence with prong multiplicity and energy sharing. Size
unknown. \textbf{(b)} Every map is flat-extrapolated above $300\,\si{\GeV}$, the last bin
edge, so a $500\,\si{\GeV}$ jet is given the $200$--$300$ bin's value verbatim. Four reasons
this is probably benign, none of them yet checked directly:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
\item \emph{Population.} Only $\sim$2.8\% of b-jets exceed $300\,\si{\GeV}$.
\item \emph{It is not where $\kl$ lives.} The $gg\rightarrow HH$ amplitude is
$\kl\times$(triangle)$\,+\,$(box), and the triangle carries an $s$-channel Higgs propagator
falling with $\hat{s}$. The $m_{HH}$ \emph{shape} difference between $\kl$ hypotheses is
therefore largest near threshold, $m_{HH}\simeq250$--$400\,\si{\GeV}$, where the destructive
interference is most modified; the high-$m_{HH}$ tail is box-dominated and comparatively
$\kl$-insensitive. b-jets from the discriminating region sit well below $300\,\si{\GeV}$.
(An earlier version of this note asserted the opposite and used it to argue the
extrapolation was dangerous; that was wrong.)
\item \emph{Flat is the right asymptote.} The v1 card's b-tag formula,
$(p_{T}>4)\times(0.904-3.53/p_{T})$, tends to $0.904$, so a constant extrapolation matches
the underlying behaviour rather than being arbitrary.
\item \emph{The escale has little left to do there.} Table~\ref{tab:response} shows the
Delphes response converging on CMS by $100$--$300\,\si{\GeV}$ ($1.035$ vs $0.970$), so the
correction is small and slowly varying where the extrapolation begins.
\end{itemize}
What would change this is a boosted or high-$m_{HH}$ category, where the extrapolated region
stops being a $3\%$ tail and becomes the signal region.

\section{The two open questions}
\label{sec:open}

\subsection{$\mtautau$}
\label{sec:mtautau}

$\mtautau=\mvis/\sqrt{x_{1}x_{2}}$ factorises, so the \emph{visible} di-$\tau$ mass $\mvis$
--- the two $\tau$ four-vectors, no $\ptmiss$, no FastMTT --- separates a $\tau$ scale error
from an $x$-fit error. The two have disjoint fixes.

\textbf{It is the four-vectors.} $\mvis$ is offset by \emph{at least} as much as $\mtautau$
(peak ratios $\approx1.24$ vs $\approx1.17$), so FastMTT is not the source; if anything it
compresses. Both legs are individually harder. With an applied scale of $\simeq0.85$ and a
residual $\simeq1.24$, the raw Delphes $\tau$ are $\sim46\%$ harder than CMS while the map
corrects $15\%$.

\textbf{Not fakes.} Splitting gen-matched from fake gives fake fractions $4.1\%$ Delphes vs
$4.3\%$ CMS with indistinguishable shapes; the gen-matched component carries everything ---
the peak displacement and an unphysical tail at $\mvis>125\,\si{\GeV}$, kinematically
impossible for $H\rightarrow\tau\tau$ and absent on CMS. Same pattern as $\dR_{\tau\tau}$.

\textbf{The response distribution.}\label{sec:response} Plotting the response (reco
$\tau$ $p_{T}$ / gen-visible-$\tau$ $p_{T}$) as a \emph{distribution}, each side divided by
its own median so the scale is removed by construction, leaves only what the map cannot
change. One control first: without a $\tau$ ID, Delphes matches a gen $\tau$ to the nearest
jet of \emph{any} kind (so a lost $\tau$-jet can match a $b$-jet), while CMS matches within
the \texttt{Tau} collection --- and mis-matching mimics the same $p_{T}$ dependence. With
the ID required on both sides the tail survives (Table~\ref{tab:response},
Figure~\ref{fig:response}):

CMS is flat in $p_{T}$ ($1.10$--$1.14$), as a calibrated object with constant fractional
resolution should be ($q_{90}$/median $=1.076\Rightarrow\sigma\simeq6\%$, right for HPS), so
the anchor is sound. Delphes is not flat and converges on CMS as $p_{T}$ rises --- what a
constant \emph{absolute} energy excess gives, large as a fraction at $25\,\si{\GeV}$ and
small at $175$. Medians converge alongside, as one cause predicts. Sharpest number: at
$20$--$30\,\si{\GeV}$ the Delphes $q_{90}$/median is $2.156$ against $1.076$ on CMS. That is
not a resolution. One caveat survives: \texttt{TauTag} is a stochastic draw uncorrelated
with contamination while DeepTau actively rejects it, so part of the gap is a
\emph{selection} correlation --- equally unreachable by a scale map, so it does not change
Section~\ref{sec:decision}, but the tail is not purely about cone size.

\textbf{Excluded.} \emph{(i) The sampled mass.} The FastMTT floor
$x_{\min}=(\mvis^{\rm leg}/\mtau)^{2}$ exceeds a typical fitted $x\simeq0.5$--$0.9$ only
above $\sim1.26\,\si{\GeV}$, so below that the mass is inert: displacing the whole low-mass
region by $200\,\si{\MeV}$ moves $\langle\mtautau\rangle$ by $0.00\%$, against $-2.4\%$ in
the $a_{1}$ tail. Replacing the 21-quantile grid with an atom at $m_{\pi^{\pm}}$ plus a
histogram CDF changes it by $0.11\%$. \emph{(ii) A symmetric resolution mismatch.} $7\%
\rightarrow25\%$ at fixed median moves the mean $0.05\%$ (RMS $+2\%$) --- but that test used
a symmetric log-normal, so it does \emph{not} cover the one-sided tail above.

\subsection{$\dR_{\tau\tau}$: a selection effect, cause unknown}

Gen-level $\dR_{\tau\tau}$ spectra agree, so the samples are the same physics; the
efficiencies differ --- Delphes selects $0.22$--$0.23$ at gen $\dR>2.5$ where CMS selects
$0.15$--$0.17$ ($\sim$3$\sigma$), with a weaker ($\sim$2$\sigma$) hint of loss below
$\dR<1$. Both push the distribution rightward. Excluded by measurement: fakes (the
discrepancy is entirely gen-matched); $\tau$-pair selection (symmetrising moved
$\dR_{\tau\tau}$, not $\mtautau$); CMS $\tau$ miscalibration (anchor response $0.97$--$0.99$).
This matters more than its size: it is a \emph{selection} effect, so no downstream
correction reaches it, and unlike $\mtautau$ we have no candidate mechanism.

\appendix
\section{Reference}

All reproducible from \texttt{delphes-pipeline}.
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0pt
\item \textbf{Cutflow} (\texttt{tauh\_cutflow.py}). Five stages --- gen visible $\tauh$,
reco, ID, cleaned jets, FastMTT --- walked identically both sides on \emph{conditional}
efficiencies. FastMTT once carried the whole deficit ($12\%$ vs $99\%$); now $1.01$ overall.
Tests inject a loss at one stage and assert it is attributed there only.
\item \textbf{FastMTT, and why the mass is sampled.} The hadronic prior vanishes unless
$x_{\min}\le1$; an AK4 jet mass ($5$--$20\,\si{\GeV}$ vs $\mtau=1.777$) admits no solution,
returning NaN and silently removing $\sim$89\% of pairs. The anchor mass fixes it, but must
be \emph{sampled}: it is a one-sided floor, so a single median relaxes a real constraint for
every leg above it, inflating the width $\sim$60\% and mean $\sim$9\% while leaving the
\emph{mode} unchanged --- a mode-gated $Z\rightarrow\tau\tau$ candle would miss it.
\item \textbf{The reference error, twice.} A \texttt{GenJet} is also a jet and also contains
UE, so it is not the analogue of \texttt{GenVisTau}. Against a true visible $\tau$
($p_{\tau}-p_{\nu_\tau}$, exact for one neutrino) the Delphes $\tau$-jet is $17\%$ harder at
$25\,\si{\GeV}$, $\sim$2\% at $250$. The same error appeared in the escale map and in the
cutflow's stage 1.
\item \textbf{Order of application.} Maps measured at CMS scale must be applied at CMS
scale; tagging raw jets evaluated a steep efficiency $\sim$17\% too far right. Applying the
scale first moved the excess $1.25\rightarrow1.18$.
\item \textbf{Gen-$\tau$ check} (\texttt{gen\_tau\_check.py}). Our visible-$\tau$
construction vs CMS \texttt{GenVisTau} on the same events: efficiency $0.991$, purity
$0.998$, stage-1 rate to $\sim$1.6\%.
\item \textbf{$\mvis$ and the split} (\texttt{nsbi\_overlay.py --diagnostics
--split-gen-matched}). $\mvis$ uses neither $\ptmiss$ nor the fit, so it attributes an
offset to one of two disjoint causes; tests pin $\mvis\le\mtautau$, invariance under
$\ptmiss$ $40\rightarrow200\,\si{\GeV}$, and exact linearity in the $\tau$ scale. The split
normalises to each side's total, so the fake curve's area is the fake fraction.
\item \textbf{Response shape} (\texttt{tau\_response.py}). Distribution per gen-$p_{T}$ bin,
lower row divided by each side's own median. Tests assert the separation: a pure scale
difference vanishes under normalisation, an injected one-sided tail survives. \texttt{--gate}
and \texttt{--require-id} expose two asymmetries in the shipped derivation (anchor gates on
gen and conditions on DeepTau; Delphes gates on reco and conditions on nothing).
\item \textbf{Map accuracy.} No map is statistics-limited at $2\times10^{5}$ anchor events
($b$-tag $0.25$--$0.42\%$/bin, $\tauh$ eff $0.43$--$1.26\%$, escale $0.2$--$0.3\%$, $\ptmiss$
$0.16$--$0.21\%$); what limits a map is $np$, not $n$. Two defects do not improve with
statistics: the mass quantile grid interpolates across the empty region between the
$\pi^{\pm}$ atom and the $\rho$ shoulder ($\sim6\times$ over-populated, measured inert), and
the $300\,\si{\GeV}$ flat extrapolation (untested).
\item \textbf{Option tradeoff analysis.} Costs by benchmark (resampling marginal cost,
HPS cone kernel, restricted-ancestry walk --- the last verified bit-identical against the
current \texttt{gen\_taus} on records with copy chains); accuracy by a common-random-number
toy propagating the measured response quantiles into $\mtautau$ for $H$/$Z$/$t\bar t$
(AUC, overlap, 1D Fisher), every headline number re-derived by an independent check.
Result in Section~\ref{sec:decision}.

\item \textbf{Generator copies.} Delphes writes the full Pythia history, CMS
\texttt{GenPart} is pruned. Rule: anything that \textbf{counts} generator objects must
collapse copies first; anything that \textbf{matches} geometrically need not.
\end{enumerate}
